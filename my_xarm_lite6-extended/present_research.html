Look, Speak, Confirm: A Human-Factors Evaluation of Complementary Multimodal Interaction for Robot Manipulation

Working title

Marius KaulMr XMr Y

Usability Lab · Rhine-Waal University of Applied Sciences

Paper in preparation Code Platform UX Robot
01Abstract

Who pays the cost of interaction?

Robot manipulation remains hard for non-experts, who often stay passive steerers that trigger scripts instead of steering intent. Multimodal interfaces promise relief, but most studies report task success and leave out the Midas Touch problem and the physiological cost to the user.

We propose a complementary allocation of interaction channels: gaze for spatial reference (“where”), speech for semantic intent (“what”) and explicit controls for confirmation and recovery (“yes / no”). A planned within-subjects study combines performance, subjective workload, eye tracking and psychophysiology (HRV, EDA) on the open UX Robot platform.

02Approach

One role per channel.

Looking and triggering stay separate: the arm moves only after an explicit confirmation.

Gaze · Tobii Pro Glasses 3Where? Spatial reference.

Gaze selects the object, it never triggers a motion.

Speech · WhisperWhat? Semantic intent.

“Pick” or “place” replaces deep GUI menus.

Controller · GUIYes or no? Confirm and recover.

A low-latency channel to confirm, cancel or take over.

Intent-safety state machine

  1. Track gazecontinuous, without effect
  2. Highlight objectnearest object to the gaze vector
  3. Dwell ≈ 300 msvisible feedback, still reversible
  4. Explicit confirmationspeech command or controller → motion
03Study design

Three conditions, one workplace.

Within-subjects, pick-and-place with rising ambiguity: similar objects, higher density.

ConditionInputRole of gaze
C1 ConventionalGUI / controller onlynone
C2 Gaze-assistedgaze selects, controller confirmsreference
C3 Complementarygaze (reference) + speech (intent) + controller (confirm, fallback)reference

Hypotheses

  1. H1 C3 shows shorter completion times than C1.
  2. H2 C3 results in lower NASA-TLX scores than C1 and C2.
  3. H3 The armed state keeps unintended activations below 5 %.
  4. H4 Lower subjective workload converges with lower physiological workload indicators.

Measures

  • Primary: task time, success rate, NASA-TLX, perceived control
  • Secondary: Midas Touch events, gaze-to-target latency, HRV (RMSSD), EDA (SCL / SCR)
  • Usability: System Usability Scale, Technology Acceptance Model
  • Exploratory: SDA-M pre / post on task representation

No results yet. Hypotheses and design are drafts; they are fixed with the supervision before the first participant.

04Contributions

What the paper adds.

  1. Architecture: a multimodal HRI design that separates spatial reference, task intent and confirmation / recovery.
  2. Intent safety: a state machine for gaze-based selection that handles temporal and spatial conflicts.
  3. Evaluation: a controlled human-factors study combining performance, workload, gaze behaviour and psychophysiology.
