Look, Speak, Confirm: A Human-Factors Evaluation of Complementary Multimodal Interaction for Robot Manipulation

01Abstract

From Passive Steerers to Dynamic Intent Steering

Resolving the cognitive mapping gap and the "Physiological Paradox" in novice robot manipulation.

Robot manipulation remains challenging for non-experts, who frequently stay passive steerers that trigger pre-defined scripts. Conventional teach pendants and nested menus impose severe extraneous cognitive load and high interaction costs.

We propose a complementary architecture where gaze references space, speech communicates task intent, and controllers confirm execution. The robot acts as a human-factors testbed to resolve the physiological paradox.

02Theoretical Foundations

The Human Cost of Human-Robot Collaboration

Bridging the Gulf of Execution under the paradigm of Industry 5.0 and Hedonomics.

Cognitive Load Theory Extraneous Load & Interaction Cost

Indirect menu hierarchies overload working memory (Sweller, 1988; Nielsen, 1994). Multiple Resource Theory (Wickens, 2002) dictates distributing demands across sensory channels.

Gaze & Midas Touch Decoupling Perception from Triggering

Eye-tracking offers rapid deictic spatial referencing. Treating fixations as direct triggers causes dangerous accidental executions (Jones et al., 2018).

Physiological Paradox Subjective Reports vs. Sympathetic Arousal

Collaborative operators often report low subjective stress despite high galvanic skin response arousal (Su et al., 2026). Objective biosignals reveal hidden strain.

Mental Models (SDA-M) Conceptual Structure vs. Tool Shortcut

Experts possess structured cognitive action networks (Schack et al., 2014). Structural Dimensional Analysis reveals whether assistance aids understanding or bypasses learning.

The Critical Research Gap

Where Engineering Fusion Meets Human Factors:

Multimodal technical fusion and human-factors evaluations rarely intersect systematically. Prior literature lacks evidence on how complementary allocation simultaneously influences performance, physiological strain, and novice mental representations.

03Interaction Architecture

One Role per Modality: Look, Speak, Confirm

Replacing redundant channel confusion with a strict division of communicative labor.

Spatial Reference (Where) Eye Gaze · Tobii Pro Glasses 3

Identifies target objects in 3D space via raycasting (RGB-D + YOLOv8). Gaze solely designates target locations and never triggers irreversible mechanical motion.

Task Intent (What) Speech · Offline Whisper

Communicates high-level semantic intentions ("Pick", "Place") without menu traversal. Grounded by temporal lead-lag synchronization where gaze precedes speech (Khan et al., 2026).

Confirmation & Override Physical Controller & Low-Latency GUI

Provides a deterministic channel for confirmation, emergency stop, and error recovery. Guarantees that physical actuation remains explicitly governed by the human.

Intent-Safety State Machine: The Cognitive Buffer

To solve the Midas Touch without introducing latency bottlenecks, the Intent-Safety Layer enforces a 4-stage deterministic transition:

  1. Visual Exploration Continuous gaze monitoring. Free ocular exploration across the workspace produces zero technical side effects.
  2. Target Raycast The object nearest to the 3D gaze vector is identified via YOLOv8 and highlighted with a subtle visual bounding cue.
  3. Dwell Buffer (≈ 300 ms) Prolonged gaze on the candidate arms the selection with clear visual feedback. Resolves gaze-shift conflicts before movement.
  4. Explicit Execution Physical trajectory execution begins strictly upon verbal command or controller press. Cancellation remains possible at any time.

Resolving Arousal Ambiguity Multimodal Sensor Convergence

Neither heart rate nor skin conductance alone distinguishes between positive engagement and cognitive distress (Tesch et al., 2021). Correlating EDA peaks with armed states maps internal strain.

Ability-Based Design Lens Accessibility Beyond Robotic Expertise

Grounded in Ability-Based Design (Wobbrock et al., 2011), the testbed isolates individual sensory-motor requirements. Evaluating novice users provides empirical baselines for assistive adaptation.

05Experimental Design

Controlled Within-Subjects Evaluation

Isolating the effects of complementary allocation under identical task autonomy and increasing spatial ambiguity.

Condition Spatial Reference ("Where") Task Intent ("What") Confirmation & Fallback Scientific Comparison
C1 Conventional (Baseline) GUI / Controller selection GUI / Teach pendant command Manual button confirm Establishes industrial baseline; assesses standard extraneous menu overhead
C2 Gaze-Assisted Gaze dwell (Armed state) GUI / Controller command Manual button confirm Isolates the specific contribution of ocular target referencing (Contrast C2 vs. C1)
C3 Complementary Gaze dwell (Armed state) Spoken natural language ("Pick") Controller confirmation & fallback Tests complete Look, Speak, Confirm paradigm (Contrasts C3 vs. C2 and C3 vs. C1)

Confirmatory Hypotheses

  1. H1 (Efficiency): Condition C3 (Complementary) will yield significantly shorter task completion times and fewer discrete interaction steps compared to C1 (Conventional).
  2. H2 (Workload): Condition C3 will result in significantly lower overall NASA-TLX workload scores compared to C1 and C2, while maintaining or improving task success rates.
  3. H3 (Intent-Safety): The multi-stage Armed state mechanism will constrain unintended activations (Midas Touch errors) to < 5% of total trials across C2 and C3.
  4. H4 (Physiological Convergence): Conditions exhibiting lower subjective workload will demonstrate converging reductions in autonomic physiological strain (increased HRV/RMSSD and reduced phasic EDA arousal).

Methodological Rigor

  • Constant Autonomy: Robot motion planning, collision avoidance, and trajectory speeds are held strictly identical across all conditions to prevent algorithmic confounds.
  • Task Ambiguity: Novice participants perform pick-and-place trials with systematically escalating clutter, distractor objects, and spatial density.
  • Counterbalancing: Condition presentation order is systematically balanced across subjects using a Balanced Latin Square design to mitigate learning and fatigue effects.
  • Planned Contrasts: Hypotheses are tested using directional Wilcoxon signed-rank tests with Holm-Bonferroni correction and linear mixed-effects modeling.
Empirical Status Notice: In accordance with research integrity standards (Skill research §0), hypotheses, measurement pipelines, and condition designs are formalized prior to participant testing. No empirical conclusions are reported until ethical clearance and experimental trials have concluded.
06Contributions & Guidelines

Generalizable Design Knowledge for Low-Barrier HRI

Translating empirical testbed findings into actionable principles for assistive and industrial manipulation.

Guideline 1 Spatiality → Gaze

Reserve eye gaze strictly for spatial deictic referencing. Gaze must never be coupled directly to irreversible mechanical motions without an explicit decoupling state.

Guideline 2 Intent → Speech

Employ spoken natural language for high-level semantic intentions ("what should happen") to bypass extraneous GUI navigation depth and eliminate spatial parameterization friction.

Guideline 3 Control → Explicit

Preserve a dedicated, low-latency, deterministic channel (controller/button) for confirmation, instantaneous cancellation, and continuous override.

Guideline 4 Safety → State-Based

Implement a multi-stage transition (Attention → Candidate → Armed → Confirmed). The Armed state provides an indispensable cognitive buffer to correct gaze-shift errors.

Guideline 5 Evaluation → Converging

Never rely on subjective surveys (NASA-TLX) alone. Synchronize objective behavioral telemetry with autonomic biomarkers (EDA/HRV) to detect hidden internal strain.

Guideline 6 Architecture → Modular

Architect multimodal platforms modularly so individual channels can be dynamically enabled, weighted, or substituted based on operator motor abilities and acoustic environment.

Core Academic Contributions of the Paper

  1. Complementary Interaction Architecture: A human-factors-driven HRI framework that partitions spatial reference (gaze), semantic intent (speech), and confirmation/recovery (controller) to resolve cognitive bottlenecks.
  2. Intent-Safety Mechanism: A deterministic state machine with calibrated dwell arming (≈300 ms) and real-time visual feedback that reliably mitigates the Midas Touch without incurring high latency costs.
  3. Multidimensional Evaluation Suite: A high-resolution HF testbed synchronizing ROS 2 machine states, ocular tracking, EDA, HRV, and psychometrics to expose the physiological paradox.
  4. Cognitive Mental Model Investigation: An exploratory SDA-M protocol revealing whether multimodal assistance supports novice development of structured Basic Action Concepts.
  5. Design Guidelines for Adaptive HRI: A set of generalizable principles for configuring low-barrier robotic interfaces across industrial and future assistive scenarios.
